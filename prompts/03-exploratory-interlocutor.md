# Research Request: Source corpus for an exploratory philosophical interlocutor

## Project context

Same Claude Code project as in `02-critic-mode-sources.md`, but covering the
*default* mode rather than the four critic-mode skills. The default mode —
"rigorous exploration" — is the agent's behavior when no specific critic
skill has been invoked.

The exploratory interlocutor:
- Engages charitably with ill-formed ideas; helps articulate vague intuitions
  into more precise formulations.
- Surfaces implicit assumptions for inspection without weaponizing them.
- Suggests what is promising or non-obvious in a half-formed thought.
- Pushes back as "here is where this might run into trouble," not as
  adversarial gotcha.
- Refuses pseudo-precision — will not pretend an idea is more developed than
  it is.
- Anti-sycophantic but not adversarial; disagreement is exploratory, not
  procedural.
- Applies lightweight argument hygiene that does not require a thesis (moves
  that work even in early-stage thinking).

This default mode is underspecified in procedure.md, which focuses on
research methodology and on the dialectical critic-mode traditions. I need
source grounding for the generative/exploratory phase of philosophical
thinking — what good philosophical exploration looks like before formal
argumentation, and how a skilled interlocutor supports it.

## What to research

Identify authoritative texts on:

1. Methodology of exploratory philosophical thinking — how philosophers
   actually think before they argue; the generative phase of philosophical
   work; the analogue (if any) to Polya's *How to Solve It* in philosophy.
2. Dialogical methods that are not yet dialectical — Aristotle's aporia
   method as exploration; the Socratic method understood as joint inquiry
   rather than refutation; modern dialogical philosophy (Bohmian dialogue,
   Gadamerian dialogue, Buberian I-Thou where it bears on philosophical
   conversation).
3. Charitable engagement with vague intuitions and ill-formed thoughts —
   methodological norms for taking seriously what is not yet articulated;
   the relationship between intuition and articulation.
4. Lightweight argument hygiene that does not require a thesis — Dennett's
   tools for thinking at the exploratory stage; conceptual analysis as
   exploration (vs. as final method); informal logic at low formalization.
5. The role of imagination, conjecture, and "philosophical taste" in
   early-stage philosophical work.
6. Pedagogical and conversational sources on philosophical dialogue —
   Mortimer Adler on philosophical conversation; working philosophers'
   notebooks, letters, or descriptions of method (Murdoch, Cavell, Anscombe,
   Russell, others where they discuss method or exhibit exploration).

## Existing baseline

Sources already on procedure.md with possible relevance: Dennett *Intuition
Pumps* (early chapters on tools for thinking), Gadamer (dialogical
hermeneutics), Pryor (writing-process guidance). Starting points, not
exhaustive.

## Output format

Same structure as `01-philosophy-methodology.md`: TL;DR, findings organized
by research area, full references with rationale, tiered acquisition list,
caveats.

Specific notes:
- This area is plausibly underdeveloped in formal methodological literature
  compared to the critic-mode methods. The research should surface that
  thinness honestly rather than overstate availability of canonical sources.
- Where canonical methodological texts are absent, working philosophers'
  descriptions of their own process (essays, interviews, notebooks) are
  acceptable substitutes if they are authoritative practitioners.
- The aim is to ground a *dialogical disposition*, not to import a system.
  Sources that exhibit good philosophical exploration in practice may be as
  valuable as sources that theorize about it.
