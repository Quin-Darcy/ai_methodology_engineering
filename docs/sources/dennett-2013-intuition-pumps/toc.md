## Preface (excerpt — what the book is)

Dennett describes the book as a record of his thinking-tools work over four decades, refined for accessibility through a 2012 Tufts seminar limited to "a dozen intrepid freshmen". The structure is deliberately modular: each numbered chapter is a self-contained tool that can be lifted and applied. The "Sources" appendix lists where each tool was first published in Dennett's earlier corpus.

## Operational map for the Mode 4 critic skill (Dennettian stress-testing)

Per Report 2, the directly extractable procedural core for a stress-testing skill is **Part II (chapters 1–12)**, the dozen general thinking tools. These work without commitment to any particular substantive thesis: Rapoport's Rules (Ch. 3), the "surely" klaxon (Ch. 10), and "deepity" detection (Ch. 12) are the most-cited diagnostic moves; Ch. 1 on making mistakes and Ch. 8 on jootsing ("jumping out of the system") are the orientation moves.

For applied examples of these tools in action against canonical thought experiments, **Part VII (Consciousness)** re-runs the Chinese Room (Ch. 60), Mary the color scientist (Ch. 64), zombies (Chs. 54–55), and heterophenomenology (Ch. 63); **Part VIII (Free Will)** has the Game of Life and the Boys from Brazil. These are the "turn the knobs" demonstrations that show what stress-testing looks like done well.

For the **exploratory mode** target use: Part II again (the lightweight diagnostic tools, especially "surely" and "deepity"), plus Ch. 75 ("Philosophy as Naïve Auto-anthropology") and Ch. 76 ("Higher-Order Truths of Chmess") for the disposition.
