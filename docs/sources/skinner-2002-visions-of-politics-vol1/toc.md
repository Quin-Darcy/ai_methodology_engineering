## General Preface (excerpt — methodological framing)

The chapters in volume 1 are all offered as contributions to the articulation and defence of one particular view about the reading and interpretation of historical texts. Skinner argues that "if we are to write the history of ideas in a properly historical style, we need to situate the texts we study within such intellectual contexts and frameworks of discourse as enable us to recognise what their authors were doing in writing them. To speak more fashionably, I emphasise the performativity of texts and the need to treat them intertextually." The aspiration is "not… to perform the impossible task of getting inside the heads of long-dead thinkers; it is simply to use the ordinary techniques of historical enquiry to grasp their concepts, to follow their distinctions, to recover their beliefs and, so far as possible, to see things their way" (p. vi).

The volume is the consolidated, revised statement of Skinner's methodology, gathering eight previously-published essays revised for republication. Chapter 4 is the revised version of the 1969 *History and Theory* essay "Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas." Skinner notes that revisions preserve "original contentions and conclusions" while improving presentation; where his current view diverges from the original, he marks the dissent in footnotes rather than altering the text (p. vi).

Volume 1 stands in a theory-and-practice relationship to Volumes 2 (*Renaissance Virtues*) and 3 (*Hobbes and Civil Science*): "In volume 1 I preach the virtues of a particular approach; in the rest of the book I try to practise what I preach" (p. vii–viii).

## Note on companion volumes (for cross-reference only)

Volume 2 (*Renaissance Virtues*, 14 chapters) and Volume 3 (*Hobbes and Civil Science*, 12 chapters) apply the methodology to specific historical material. Their chapter listings appear in the Full Contents (pp. ix–x) of this volume but are out of scope for the Mode 3 critic skill, whose target is the methodological apparatus in Volume 1.
