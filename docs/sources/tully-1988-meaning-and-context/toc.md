## Operational map for the Mode 3 (Skinnerian) critic skill

Per Report 2, Tully's volume is "uniquely useful as both implementation showcase and critique compilation" and "for Mode 3 specifically, this is the single most usable single-volume source" — because each Skinnerian methodological move comes paired with an objection and Skinner's counter-move.

The dialogical pattern that makes this volume operationally distinctive:

1. **Skinner sets out the method** (Chs. 2–6, esp. Ch. 2 with the four "mythologies" taxonomy and Ch. 3 on motives/intentions).
2. **Critics push back** (Chs. 7–13). Particularly:
   - Hollis (Ch. 7) — limits of speech-act analysis applied to history.
   - Femia (Ch. 9) — historicist objection to "revisionist" methods.
   - Taylor (Ch. 13) — "The hermeneutics of conflict": when interpretive contexts themselves contain irreducible conflict.
3. **Skinner replies** (Ch. 14, "A reply to my critics") — three internal sections defending the method, the analysis of beliefs, and the speech-act framework.

For dialogical-move extraction the highest-value combination is **Ch. 2 (the original method statement) + the Hollis/Taylor critiques (Chs. 7, 13) + Skinner's reply (Ch. 14)**. This trio gives a candidate-directive pattern alongside its principled objection alongside Skinner's counter — exactly the move/counter-move material a Mode 3 skill needs in order to engage critically rather than just apply a method.

For the **research procedure** (also marked in `_manifest/proc.md`), the Skinner essays in Part II are the same texts the procedure references for the principle of charity, contextualism, and the four mythologies — though for those the consolidated 2002 *Visions of Politics* Vol. 1 versions are preferable for citation, with this volume providing the unrevised originals plus the critical apparatus.
