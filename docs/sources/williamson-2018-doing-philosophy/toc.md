## Operational map for the research-procedure deliverable

Williamson's chapters correspond closely to philosophical methodology categories. For Tier 1 use in the research-procedure document, the most operative chapters:

- **Ch. 2 ("Starting from Common Sense", p. 6)** — frames philosophy as continuous with ordinary curiosity; methodologically pluralistic against extreme rationalism and extreme empiricism.
- **Ch. 3 ("Disputing", p. 19)** — the dialectical character of philosophical work: dispute as the engine of clarification.
- **Ch. 4 ("Clarifying Terms", p. 35)** — conceptual analysis and its limits; what counts as a useful definition.
- **Ch. 5 ("Doing Thought Experiments", p. 50)** — Williamson's accessible treatment of thought-experiment methodology (the more technical version is in *The Philosophy of Philosophy*, 2007).
- **Ch. 6 ("Comparing Theories", p. 66)** — theory-comparison criteria in philosophy: simplicity, fruitfulness, scope.
- **Ch. 7 ("Deducing", p. 82)** — the role of formal deduction.
- **Ch. 8 ("Using the History of Philosophy", p. 98)** — Williamson's stance on historical material (an analytic-friendly version that pairs interestingly with the Skinner/Rorty pair).
- **Ch. 9 ("Using Other Fields", p. 111)** — interdisciplinarity.
- **Ch. 10 ("Model-Building", p. 127)** — the specifically Williamsonian methodological move, applied as a generalizing pattern.

For an LLM research-procedure document, this book is a high-density source of self-contained methodological directives — each chapter is short and concrete enough that extraction will yield well-formed candidate directives with minimal qualification preservation work.
