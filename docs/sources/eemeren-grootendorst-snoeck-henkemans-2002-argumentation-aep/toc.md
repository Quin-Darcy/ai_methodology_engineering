## Each chapter follows a uniform structure

Per the Preface: each chapter has "Essentials" (the substantive theory) → "Further reading" → "Exercises" (for practice). Several chapters also include "Special assignments" with extended worked examples drawn from real discourse (newspaper editorials, op-eds, Calvin and Hobbes strips, policy debates).

This is the **textbook companion** to van Eemeren & Grootendorst's *A Systematic Theory of Argumentation* (CUP 2004), written by the same Amsterdam group. Where the *Systematic Theory* is the consolidated theoretical statement, this book is the operational textbook walking through reconstruction step-by-step on extended examples.

## Operational map for the Mode 1 (Pragma-Dialectical) critic skill

Per Report 2, this is **the single highest-value implementation source for Mode 1** — the only English-language pragma-dialectical textbook by the originators with worked applications.

Most operative chapters for dialogical-move extraction:

- **Ch. 1 (Differences of Opinion, p. 3)** — how to recognize what's actually in dispute (the trigger condition for the entire pragma-dialectical procedure).
- **Ch. 2 (Argumentation and Discussion, p. 23)** — the four-stage model of critical discussion (confrontation → opening → argumentation → concluding).
- **Ch. 4 (Unexpressed Standpoints and Premises, p. 49)** — the reconstruction moves: how to surface what's left implicit. This is the most directly extractable "dialogical move" content.
- **Ch. 5 (Structure of Argumentation, p. 63)** — multiple/coordinative/subordinative analysis; schematic representation.
- **Ch. 7 + Ch. 8 (Fallacies)** — fallacies as violations of the ten rules. Each section maps to a specific rule.

The "Overview of Rules for Critical Discussion and Fallacies" (p. 182) is a one-page summary of the canonical ten rules — useful as a reference card.

The Special Assignments (1–8 across the book) provide the worked examples that make this source pedagogically distinct from the *Systematic Theory*.
