## Foreword (Midgley) — excerpt on what the book does

Midgley's Foreword to the Great Minds edition opens with the anecdote of Murdoch's flat 1942 verdict ("X is a good man and Y is a bad man") as "an accurate but grotesquely unfashionable judgement." She frames the book's polemical target precisely:

> The trouble at that time was not just that moral judgments like this were constantly being attacked, dismissed as mere puffs of emotion or mindless aspirations. It was that the inner self who engages in these judgments — the essential person, the active self who really matters — was somehow being ignored and forgotten.

She quotes Murdoch on the active mechanism the book counters:

> We are anxiety-ridden animals. Our minds are continually active, fabricating an anxious, usually self-pre-occupied, often falsifying veil which partially conceals the world. (p. 82)

And the famous corrective image:

> I am looking out of my window in an anxious and resentful state of mind, oblivious of my surroundings, brooding perhaps on some damage done to my prestige. Then suddenly I observe a hovering kestrel. In a moment everything is altered. The brooding self with its hurt vanity has disappeared. There is nothing now but kestrel. (p. 82)

The Foreword positions the book against (i) emotivism / non-cognitivism in 1950s/60s analytic ethics, (ii) Sartrean existentialism's choosing self, and (iii) post-Wittgensteinian behaviourism. Murdoch's positive position: morality is not principally a matter of *willing* (choosing actions) but of *attention* — looking justly at what is there, including persons, until selfhood-distorting projections recede.

Per the manifest, **Ch. 1 ("The Idea of Perfection", pp. 1–44)** is the operational target. This essay is the canonical statement of Murdoch's "attention" thesis and contains the worked example (the M / D case — a mother revising her perception of her daughter-in-law D over years) that is the most directly transferable image in the volume for the exploratory disposition. Attention as a moral-philosophical accomplishment — sustained, patient, anti-projective looking — is precisely the disposition the exploratory-default CLAUDE.md is meant to enact in conversation: charitable engagement with the user's idea as it actually is, not as one's first reaction projects it.

- **Ch. 3 ("The Sovereignty of Good Over Other Concepts", pp. 75–101)** — synthesizes the position; positions the Good against alternative central moral concepts (freedom, the will). Useful as the formal statement of where the previous essays land.
