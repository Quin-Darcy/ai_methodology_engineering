## What the essay does (excerpt)

Pryor's stated framing: "Philosophical writing is different from the writing you'll be asked to do in other courses." The essay then enumerates concrete moves and antipatterns under each section header.

§1 ("What Does One Do in a Philosophy Paper?") opens with a numbered set of substantive directives:
1. A philosophy paper consists of the reasoned defense of some claim.
2. A good philosophy paper is modest and makes a small point; but it makes that point clearly and straightforwardly, and it offers good reasons in support of it.
3. (continues with further numbered points throughout the essay)

These numbered points are the essay's primary unit of guidance — each is a self-contained directive followed by its rationale and concrete examples.

## Operational map for the research-procedure deliverable (and exploratory)

This essay is the most-cited online guide for narrowing a philosophical thesis. Its core directive — "A good philosophy paper is modest and makes a small point; but it makes that point clearly and straightforwardly, and it offers good reasons in support of it" — operationalizes "well-formed thesis" in a way that maps directly onto the research-procedure document's question-formulation stage and the exploratory mode's "anti-pseudo-precision" disposition.

For direct extraction, the entire essay is short enough to treat as a single chunk; the numbered directives within §1 and §2 are the primary candidates. The "Minor Points" section (§3) contains concrete writing-craft directives (grammar, structure, prose conventions) of secondary value.

For Pryor's parallel guide ("Guidelines on Reading Philosophy"), see jimpryor.net/teaching/guidelines/reading.html — not currently in `docs/sources/` but may be worth acquiring as a companion essay if reading-side directives are wanted.
