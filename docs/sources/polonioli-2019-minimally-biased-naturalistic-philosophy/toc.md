## Abstract (verbatim)

> Naturalistic philosophers rely on literature search and review in a number of ways and for different purposes. Yet this article shows how the products of literature search and review are likely to be unreliable, due to the systematic impact of cognitive biases and limited information processing capacities. The article also shows how this is consistent with intriguing evidence concerning the impact of biases on the products of literature search and review in disciplines other than philosophy. The article finally argues that systematic reviews and meta-analyses provide tools to limit such impact and that there are good reasons to believe that they should be used by naturalistic philosophers more extensively.

**Keywords:** Cognitive bias · Heuristics · Naturalism · Systematic review · Narrative review · Methodology.

## Operational map for the research-procedure deliverable

This article is the most direct prior call for transposing systematic-review methodology into philosophical work. Its argumentative structure exactly mirrors a problem the procedure document is designed to solve: naturalistic / metaphilosophical work relies on literature surveys, which are subject to the same biases that systematic-review methodology was developed to mitigate. The article's contribution to the procedure document is twofold:

- **Diagnosis of the problem (§3, esp. §3.1)** — confirmation, salience, availability, and false-consensus biases in narrative literature reviews; relevant for justifying why a procedure document is needed at all.
- **Practical recommendations (§§4.3, 5.4)** — concrete proposals for what a "minimally biased" philosophical literature review looks like; directly relevant to the procedure document's stage definitions.
- **Feasibility and objections (§§4.4, 5.2, 5.3)** — anticipates objections to importing health-sciences methodology into philosophy (e.g., "philosophical claims aren't quantifiable", "the sources aren't homogeneous"); useful for the procedure document's framing of *why* and *how far* to import the methodology.

Pair with `arksey-omalley-2005-scoping-studies` (the framework Polonioli is implicitly recommending), `tricco-2018-prisma-scr` (reporting standard), and `sofaer-strech-2012-systematic-reviews-of-reasons` (the closest existing model of a systematic review of arguments).
