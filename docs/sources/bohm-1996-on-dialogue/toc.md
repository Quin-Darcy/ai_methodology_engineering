## Foreword (excerpt — what the book is)

> *On Dialogue* is the most comprehensive documentation to date of the dialogical world view of David Bohm. … This revised and expanded edition of the original booklet of the same name is intended to serve as both a practical working manual for those interested in dialogue and a theoretical foundation for those interested in probing into the deeper implications of Bohm's dialogical world view.

Nichol's Foreword frames the operational core: dialogue as distinct from discussion (Ch. 2's etymological move — "dialogue" from *dia-logos*, "meaning through"; "discussion" from the same root as "percussion" and "concussion"). Bohm's dialogue is a non-agenda-driven sustained group practice in which assumptions are *suspended* — neither acted on nor suppressed — so that participants can become aware of the "fragmentary and self-destructive nature of many of our thought processes." Three concepts the Foreword emphasizes as the disposition's load-bearing notions:

- **Suspension** — a third stance distinct from acting on an assumption and from defending against it; allowing the assumption to "reveal itself" so its structure (including its bodily/affective texture) can be observed (Ch. 6).
- **Proprioception of thought** — Bohm's analogy with proprioception of the body: noticing thought as it occurs, including the reflexive "I" that mistakes itself for the observer (Chs. 5–6).
- **Participatory thought** — a non-objectifying mode of thinking in which the thinker is in the world thought about, not standing outside it; recovers a stance Bohm sees as occluded by post-Enlightenment "literal" thought (Ch. 7).

## Operational map for the exploratory-default deliverable

Per the manifest, this is the single most direct match in the corpus for the exploratory-default disposition. The relevant operational moves cluster across three chapters:

- **Ch. 2 ("On Dialogue", pp. 6–47)** — the canonical statement of the dialogue/discussion distinction; the absence of pre-set agenda; "stream of meaning flowing among and through us and between us"; the practice of attending to one's own "blocks." This chapter is the core for the exploratory disposition's stance toward conversation: charitable, non-purposive, attentive.
- **Ch. 5 ("The Observer and the Observed", pp. 69–71)** — the discipline of noticing how assumptions structure observation; pairs naturally with the slim-core CLAUDE.md's anti-sycophancy provisions and the lightweight Dennettian hygiene rules (the "surely" klaxon).
- **Ch. 6 ("Suspension, the Body, and Proprioception", pp. 72–83)** — the most operationally specific chapter; Bohm's three-way distinction between acting on a reaction, suppressing it, and *suspending* it gives the disposition a precise stance toward strong claims and disagreements. Suspension as the alternative to either confrontation or capitulation is directly transferrable to a CLAUDE.md instruction set.

Chs. 1, 3, 4, and 7 provide the wider conceptual frame (fragmentation as a disease of thought; participatory thought as an alternative ontology) and are useful for grounding language in the slim-core but are less directly extractable as procedural directives.

For directive extraction, **Chs. 2, 5, 6** are the strongest candidates; chunking by chapter (one chunk per file) is the natural granularity given chapter sizes (Ch. 2 is by far the longest at ~42 pages).
