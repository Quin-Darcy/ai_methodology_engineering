## What the volume is

*Culture and Value* (German title *Vermischte Bemerkungen* — "Mixed Remarks") is a selection of remarks from Wittgenstein's *Nachlass* that von Wright extracted as not directly belonging to the philosophical works (e.g., not part of the *Investigations*, *Zettel*, *On Certainty*, *Remarks on Colour*, etc.) but are nonetheless philosophically significant. The body is arranged chronologically by year of composition (1914 onward), not thematically.

From the Foreword (verbatim, ed. 1977 — von Wright):

> In the manuscript material left by Wittgenstein there are numerous notes which do not belong directly with his philosophical works although they are scattered among the philosophical texts. Some of these notes are autobiographical, some are about the nature of philosophical activity, and some concern subjects of a general sort, such as questions about art or about religion.

> ... by and large, splitting up the material in this way would probably give an impression of artificiality. ... For many of Wittgenstein's most impressive "aphorisms" are to be found in his philosophical works — in the Notebooks from the First World War, in the Tractatus, and in the Investigations too. I should like to say that it is when they are embedded in such contexts that Wittgenstein's remarks really have their most powerful effect. But for that very reason it did not seem to me right to tear them from their surroundings.

## Operational map for the exploratory-default deliverable

Per the manifest, this volume is in scope for **late remarks on philosophical method, style, and examples** — material that does not appear in the *Investigations* but that supplements its methodological remarks (PI §§89–133) with first-personal observations on what philosophical work felt like to Wittgenstein. The high-leverage remarks are scattered across the volume rather than concentrated in a single section, so extraction will require either:

- A targeted pass focused on remarks tagged "philosophy" or "method" — e.g., remarks on philosophical style ("If I am thinking just for myself ..."); on the difficulty of beginning a thought; on what writing philosophy is doing; on examples; on simile and aspect.
- Or a full read with marginal notation, since the volume is short (~110 English pages of body) and the dating ordering rewards a chronological read.

Notable thematic clusters relevant to the disposition:

- **Remarks on the nature of philosophical work** — Wittgenstein's recurring image of philosophy as a struggle to see clearly something already in front of one; the resistance to "explaining" in favor of "describing".
- **Remarks on style** — the stylistic-as-philosophical (e.g., comparison with the literary fragment; aphorism as a philosophical genre; the connection between *what* one is saying and *how* one says it).
- **Remarks on examples** — Wittgenstein's recurring methodological commitment that "the example is the work" (cf. PI §122 on perspicuous representation); examples in *Culture and Value* often gloss what an example is *doing* in the *Investigations*.
- **Remarks on Shakespeare, Tolstoy, Russell, Frege** — interpretive judgments that show how Wittgenstein reads other thinkers; useful for grounding the disposition's stance toward charitable interpretation.

For directive extraction, the volume is best chunked by *period* (e.g., 1929–1934, 1937–1941, 1945–1948, 1949–1951) rather than by chapter — there are no chapter divisions. A v1 extraction pass should focus on the philosophy-of-philosophy remarks; a v2 pass can take in the broader material on art, religion, and culture if those prove useful for the disposition's framing of how a question is asked.

Pair with `wittgenstein-1953-philosophical-investigations` (especially Part I §§89–133, Part II §xi) and `cavell-1979-claim-of-reason` Part IV (which reads Wittgenstein's reflections on his own writing as continuous with the philosophical work).
