## Abstract (verbatim)

> There are many ethical decisions in the practice of health research and care. Decision-makers, including bioethicists, who are skeptical of approaching such decisions on the basis of their personal opinions or initial intuitions need empirical investigation and/or systematic reviews of empirical investigations of the relevant facts, and ethical analysis and synthesis of the relevant reasons. The recent boom in 'empirical ethics' has increased the volume and quality of empirical investigations and systematic reviews of empirical investigations relevant to bioethics. However, there is a paucity of systematic reviews of reason-based ethics literature. McCullough and colleagues have set out a model for writing systematic reviews of reason-based bioethics. We will argue that this model is inadequate and propose our own. The key feature of our 'systematic review of reasons' is that it presents a comprehensive, critical and transparent retrieval and synthesis of reasons (as opposed to ethical conclusions) given in the literature. We will argue that systematic reviews of reasons are necessary for both bioethical research and bioethically informed decision-making.

## Operational map for the research-procedure deliverable

This is the closest existing model in the literature for what the procedure document is trying to build: a *systematic review of arguments* rather than of empirical findings. The article's significance is twofold.

- **The McCullough Model critique (Introduction §4 + The Need §1)** — the McCullough Model takes the same research question as a classical systematic review (an ethical conclusion is "all-things-considered true") and assigns a single quality score to each publication's reasoning. Sofaer & Strech argue this conflates argument validity with all-things-considered truth, and that scoring reasoning quality on a 0/0.5/1 scale fails because (a) it can't distinguish valid-from-false-premises from valid-and-sound, (b) it can't separate good treatment of one argument from poor treatment of another in the same publication, (c) Cochrane itself recommends against summary scores. Directly relevant to the procedure document's design choices around quality assessment and synthesis.
- **The alternative model (The Need §2)** — the research question is *what reasons have been given*, not *what should one conclude*. Reviewers extract reasons rather than conclusions; no aggregate quality score is produced. This is the model the procedure document should adopt by default for philosophical questions, since philosophical conclusions are typically contested rather than provisionally settled.
- **The "Why" defenses (The Need §§3–5)** — anticipates the objection that systematic reviewing of reasons is either unnecessary (informal review suffices) or impossible (philosophy isn't measurable). The replies are directly transferrable to procedure-document framing.
