## The 20-item PRISMA-ScR checklist (the operational core)

The checklist is the deliverable's reason to exist. Each item is one row in the published table; the Appendix (online) provides an explanation and elaboration of each.

### Title
1. **Title** — Identify the report as a scoping review.

### Abstract
2. **Structured summary** — Provide a structured summary including background, objectives, eligibility criteria, sources of evidence, charting methods, results, and conclusions related to the review questions and objectives.

### Introduction
3. **Rationale** — Describe the rationale for the review in the context of what is already known. Explain why the review questions/objectives lend themselves to a scoping review approach.
4. **Objectives** — Provide an explicit statement of the questions and objectives being addressed with reference to their key elements (e.g., population/participants, concepts, and context — PCC).

### Methods
5. **Protocol and registration** — Indicate whether a review protocol exists; state if and where it can be accessed; provide registration information if available.
6. **Eligibility criteria** — Specify characteristics of sources used as eligibility criteria (e.g., years, language, publication status), with a rationale.
7. **Information sources** — Describe all information sources in the search (databases with dates of coverage, author contact, etc.) and the date the most recent search was executed.
8. **Search** — Present the full electronic search strategy for at least one database, including any limits used, such that it could be repeated.
9. **Selection of sources of evidence** — State the process for selecting sources (screening and eligibility) included in the scoping review.
10. **Data charting process** — Describe the methods of charting data (e.g., calibrated forms, independence/duplication) and any processes for obtaining and confirming data from investigators.
11. **Data items** — List and define all variables for which data were sought and any assumptions and simplifications made.
12. **Critical appraisal of individual sources of evidence** — If done, provide a rationale, describe methods, and explain how this information was used in any data synthesis. (Optional for scoping reviews.)
13. **Summary measures** — Not applicable for scoping reviews.
14. **Synthesis of results** — Describe the methods of handling and summarizing the data that were charted.
15. **Risk of bias across studies** — Not applicable for scoping reviews.
16. **Additional analyses** — Not applicable for scoping reviews.

### Results
17. **Selection of sources of evidence** — Give numbers screened, assessed, and included, with reasons for exclusions at each stage (ideally a flow diagram).
18. **Characteristics of sources of evidence** — For each source, present the characteristics for which data were charted and provide the citations.
19. **Critical appraisal within sources of evidence** — If done, present data on critical appraisal (see item 12).
20. **Results of individual sources of evidence** — For each included source, present the relevant data charted that relate to the review questions and objectives.
21. **Synthesis of results** — Summarize and/or present the charting results as they relate to the review questions and objectives.
22. **Risk of bias across studies** — Not applicable for scoping reviews.
23. **Additional analyses** — Not applicable for scoping reviews.

### Discussion
24. **Summary of evidence** — Summarize the main results (including overview of concepts, themes, types of evidence available); link to the review questions and objectives; consider the relevance to key groups.
25. **Limitations** — Discuss the limitations of the scoping-review process.
26. **Conclusions** — Provide a general interpretation of the results with respect to the review questions and objectives, as well as potential implications and/or next steps.

### Funding
27. **Funding** — Describe sources of funding for the included sources of evidence and for the scoping review; describe the role of the funders.

(Items 13, 15, 16, 22, 23 are explicitly NOT applicable to scoping reviews. The 20 essential items therefore comprise items 1–12, 14, 17–21, 24–27.)

## Operational map for the research-procedure deliverable

This is the canonical reporting checklist for scoping reviews — the format and field set the procedure document's reports should approximate (with appropriate translations away from health-sciences vocabulary). Specific points of leverage:

- **Items 3–4 (Rationale, Objectives, with PCC framing)** — directly relevant for the procedure document's question-formulation stage; PCC (population/concepts/context) maps onto a philosophical-question taxonomy.
- **Items 5–11 (Protocol, Eligibility, Information sources, Search, Selection, Charting, Items)** — the operational core. The procedure document's source-ecology guidance, search strategy, and inclusion criteria should be expressible in these slots.
- **Item 12 (Critical appraisal)** — explicitly optional for scoping reviews. Reinforces the Sofaer/Strech argument that quality scoring is not load-bearing for a review of arguments.
- **Items 17–21 (Results)** — reinforce that the report's job is to *map* the literature, not to deliver a verdict; pairs with the "scaffolding not authority" framing in the slim-core custom instructions.
- **Items 24–26 (Discussion)** — the synthesis stage; format roughly matches the "weak claims and remaining uncertainty" + "what to read next" sections the procedure already mandates.

Pair with `arksey-omalley-2005-scoping-studies` (the underlying methodology), `sofaer-strech-2012-systematic-reviews-of-reasons` (extension to argument-reviews), and `polonioli-2019-minimally-biased-naturalistic-philosophy` (philosophical motivation).
