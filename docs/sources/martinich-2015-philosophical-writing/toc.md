## Operational map for the research-procedure deliverable

This is the standard textbook treatment of philosophical writing, organized around the practical sequence of moving from topic to thesis to defended argument. Each chapter reads as a set of operational directives for one stage of writing.

For Tier 1 use in the research-procedure document, the most operative chapters:

- **Ch. 2 (Logic and Argument for Writing)** — formal apparatus the procedure document can build on for "how to evaluate a candidate argument."
- **Ch. 3 (Structure of a Philosophical Essay)** — direct candidate for the procedure document's output-template stage.
- **Ch. 5 (Tactics for Analytic Writing)** — concrete moves (definitions, distinctions, analysis, dilemmas, scenarios, counterexamples, reductio, dialectical reasoning). Each sub-section is a self-contained directive set.
- **Ch. 6 (Constraints on Content)** — pursuit of truth, use of authority, burden of proof. Directly relevant to the procedure's anti-sycophancy and citation-discipline directives.
- **Ch. 7 (Goals of Form)** — coherence, clarity, conciseness, rigor — formal output-quality criteria.
- **Ch. 9 (How to Read a Philosophical Work)** — reading-side moves; pairs with Pryor.

Edition note: the 4th edition (2015) is what this PDF contains. The 5th edition (2024) added chapters on belief networks and properties of sound interpretations. For citation, flag edition-specific chapter numbering when quoting.
